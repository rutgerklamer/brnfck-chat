Read a line into memory

Reads letters and stores them in cells next to each other, up to a newline or the end of the input.

Watch it run

The form “Up to a newline” works on 7 cells; the named ones are “zero”, “letter” and “end”. Press Play, drag the step slider forwards and back, or edit the line:

One cell per letter, from cell 1 on. Cell 0 stays zero and the cell after the last letter is zero: a string in brainfuck is a row of non-zero cells between two zeros.

Those two zeros are what make the string usable. [<] walks to its start and [>] to its end, whatever its length: see find a zero cell or a marker.

To stop at a newline, take 10 off each letter as it arrives. A newline becomes zero and ends the loop; any other letter gets its 10 back.

What it needs

  • A zero cell before the string and enough zero cells after it.
  • The newline form needs wrapping cells and an input that has a newline in it.
  • The end-of-input form needs , to give 0 (or leave the cell alone) at the end of the input.
Common mistakes
  • Starting on cell 0. Then there is no zero to the left of the string and [<] runs off the tape.
  • Input without a final newline in the newline form: at the end of the input it reads zeros for ever.
