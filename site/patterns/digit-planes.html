Write many numbers compactly as digits

Puts a long row of fixed numbers on the tape with a few characters per number, instead of one plus sign per unit.

Watch it run

The form “Three digit places in base 5” works on 10 cells; the named ones are “number 0”, “number 1”, “number 2”, “number 3”, “number 4” and “number 5”. Press Play, drag the step slider forwards and back, or edit the numbers (13 to 262):

A number written as plus signs costs its own value in characters. Written in digits it costs a few characters per digit: 200 is 1, 3, 0 in base 5 with digits from −2 to 2.

The row is written one digit place at a time, for all numbers at once. The text walks along the row and gives every cell its small top digit. Then one loop, [[-<+++++>]>], multiplies every cell by 5 into its left neighbour. Then the next digit is added, and so on.

That loop tests the cell it stands on to know whether the row has ended. So no cell may be zero on the way: every number starts at 1, and the digits are chosen with that in mind.

What it needs

  • The row ends up three cells to the left of where it is first written: one cell per digit place.
  • A zero cell after the row. Cells wide enough for the numbers (here above 255, so 16 or 32 bits).
  • With this simple form the numbers must lie between 13 and 262.
Common mistakes
  • A number that passes through zero between two digit places: the multiplying loop stops there and the rest of the row is lost.
  • Thinking this saves steps. It saves characters; the steps are about the same.
