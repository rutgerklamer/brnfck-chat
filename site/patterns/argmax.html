Watch it run

The form “Two digits of four bits” works on 31 cells; the named ones are “result 0”, “high 0”, “low 0”, “result 1”, “high 1”, “low 1”, “result 2”, “high 2”, “low 2”, “result 3”, “high 3”, “low 3” and “scratch”. Press Play, drag the step slider forwards and back, or edit the scores:

Add 1 to every number at the same time, again and again. The first one to reach the top was the largest. "Add 1 to all" is one flat loop: one step, however many numbers there are.

"Has anyone arrived?" is a scan (see find a zero cell or a marker). The numbers are stored as "distance still to go", so an arrival is a zero cell, and [>>>>>] slides over all the others and stops on it. If nobody has arrived it stops on an empty frame at the end, and a marker there says "go round again".

A race over the whole value would take up to 256 rounds. So each number is split into two digits of 4 bits. The race is run on the high digits first, 16 rounds at most; only the winners of that race take part in the race on the low digits.

The first of several equal numbers wins, because the scan comes from the left.

What it needs

  • Each number in a frame of five cells: marker, marker, result, high digit, low digit, with one empty frame before and after. One scratch cell.
  • Any cell size that wraps. The numbers are used up; the result is a 1 in the result cell of the winner.
  • To be fast it needs an interpreter that does a flat loop in one step. In plain steps the shifts are long loops and the gain is lost.
Common mistakes
  • Racing on whole values. With 512 numbers a round is about 515 steps; 200 rounds are 100,000 steps.
  • Forgetting the empty frames at both ends: the scan needs a place to stop, and the walk home needs one too.
