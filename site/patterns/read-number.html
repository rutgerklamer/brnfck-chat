Watch it run

The form “A line of digits” works on 3 cells, named “number”, “letter” and “scratch”. Press Play, drag the step slider forwards and back, or change the number typed with the slider:

The digits arrive first to last. For every new digit: multiply what we have by 10, and add the digit. 1, then 17, then 173.

A digit arrives as a letter code, 48 more than its value. So 48 is taken off first.

Multiplying by 10 is the pattern multiply by a constant: a flat loop, one optimised step.

What it needs

  • Short form: a line that ends with a newline, digits only; two scratch cells; wrapping cells.
  • The form by Urban Müller also stops at the end of the input, with any of the three answers , can give there.
  • A number that does not fit the cell wraps.
Common mistakes
  • Feeding it anything but digits. Both forms treat every letter as a digit: "a" counts as 49.
  • Typing 300 on 8-bit cells and expecting 300. The cell holds 300 − 256 = 44.
