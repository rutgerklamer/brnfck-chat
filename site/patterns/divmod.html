Divide, with remainder

Divides one cell by another, or by a constant, and gives the quotient and the remainder.

Division is counting: take 1 from n and 1 from the divisor, again and again. Every time the divisor reaches zero, one more "whole" has been taken out: add 1 to the quotient and fill the divisor up again.

To fill it up again the loop keeps a second count that goes up while the divisor goes down. That count is also the remainder when n runs out.

The clever part is the pointer. [>+>>] and the loop after it are an if and else on the divisor that end on the same cell both ways.

Why this is costly. One round per unit of n, and the loop is not flat. Dividing 1,000,000 takes about 7 million optimised steps. For a divisor that is a power of two there is a much faster way: shift, round and clamp.

What it needs

  • Main form: the six cells n, d, 1, 0, 0, 0. The pattern puts the 1 there itself.
  • The divisor must be at least 1. A divisor of 0 acts as "cell size" (256 on 8-bit cells).
  • Any cell size. n and d are used up.
Common mistakes
  • Using the well-known shorter loop with a divisor of 1. It fails: see the note "Wrong as often given" under this list.
  • Reading the raw result: before the tidy-up the cells hold d − remainder and remainder + 1, not d and the remainder.

Wrong as often given

The shorter loop that is usually quoted, [->-[>+>>]>[+[-<+>]>+>>]<<<<<], is correct for a divisor of 2 or more. With a divisor of 1 the pointer walks off to the left. The wiki says so; many copies of the snippet do not. The main form of this page (the first figure) is the wiki's "fixed version", which starts the remainder cell at 1.

Each wrong form is run, and it fails in exactly this way.

Where it is used

  • Print a number in decimal divides by 10 once per digit.
  • Build toward a chatbot, lesson 4, "Only one question", measures how slow dividing by counting is.
  • The chat program never divides this way: it multiplies by a reciprocal from a table (see table lookup as a decision tree) and shifts (see shift, round and clamp).
