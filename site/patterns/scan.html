The form “The first zero to the right” works on 6 cells, named “cell 0”, “cell 1”, “cell 2”, “cell 3”, “cell 4” and “zero”. Press Play, drag the step slider forwards and back, or edit cells:

[>] is a loop whose whole body is one step to the right. It asks "is this cell zero?" and, if not, moves on. It stops on the first zero cell.

To stop on another value, change the question: take the value off, ask, and put it back before moving on.

A scan is the one loop here that does not come back to where it started. The program no longer knows the number of the cell it stands on, only what kind of cell it is. That is how brainfuck does without addresses.

What it needs

  • There must be a cell to stop on, or the pointer runs off the tape.
  • The marker forms need wrapping cells (0 − 1 has to be a number).
  • The scan for a non-zero cell needs one spare zero cell to the left of where it starts.
Common mistakes
  • Data that may contain a zero inside a [>] scan: the scan stops there.
  • Starting the non-zero scan on the first cell of the tape. As usually given, it says only "the start cell must be zero"; it also touches the cell to the left of it.

Where it is used

  • Every walk over data ends in a scan: a row of records (walk an array of records), a string (read a line into memory).
  • Pick the largest by a race uses a scan as its "did anyone reach zero?" test.
  • Build toward a chatbot, lesson 7, "No addresses", is about working without addresses.
