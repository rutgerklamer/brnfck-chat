Watch it run

The form “A value from 0 to 255” works on 9 cells; the named ones are “x”. Press Play, drag the step slider forwards and back, or change x with the slider:

A cell holds a number; the screen wants letters. The digits are found by dividing by 10 (see divide, with remainder): the remainder is the last digit, the quotient is the rest.

So the last digit comes first. The digits are stored and then printed in the other order. Adding 48 turns a digit into its letter.

The 8-bit form knows there are at most three digits and writes the two divisions out. The form for any cell size loops: divide, store a digit, go on with the quotient until it is zero, then walk back printing (see find a zero cell or a marker).

What it needs

  • 8-bit form: eight cells; the value is kept; made for cells that hold 0 to 255.
  • Any-size form: the value, a zero cell, then about three cells per digit, all cleared at the end. The value is kept.
  • A single digit needs one scratch cell.
Common mistakes
  • Printing the cell itself with .: that prints the letter with that code, not the number.
  • Forgetting that the cost grows with the value, because division counts. Printing 1,000,000 on 32-bit cells takes millions of steps.
