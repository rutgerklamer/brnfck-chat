Try three commands

    
You can see the same commands at work without loading a model. Brainfuck keeps its numbers in a row of memory cells, and a pointer marks the cell it is working on.

    
Press , to read the letter A into the cell. The cell becomes 65, the number computers use for A. Press + to add one, then . to print.

    
    
The cell holds 66, so the program prints B. The chat model uses these same commands on a much longer row of cells.

Numbers first, then words

    
The program has two parts. The first writes the model's numbers into memory. In brnfck-qwen2.5-1.5b, the largest program, that is 11,334,697,744 of its 11,430,468,880 characters. It reads nothing and prints nothing.

    
    
The second part, the engine, reads your question and uses those numbers to work out which piece of text should come next. It prints that piece, then does the same again with the new piece included. An answer grows one piece at a time.

    
    
The program does not train a model. It runs one that Hugging Face or Alibaba Cloud trained earlier.

Five programs, five costs

    
There are four programs of text. brnfck-smollm2-135m loads quickest and often gets simple questions wrong. The larger ones need more memory and take longer for every piece of an answer. A fifth, brnfck-qwen3.5-0.8b-vision, is brnfck-qwen3.5-0.8b with the part of its model that looks at pictures: it reads a photo before the question. Looking at a picture shows how. A sixth, brnfck-qwen2.5-coder-0.5b, answers with a brainfuck program of its own, which runs under its answer: Brainfuck that writes brainfuck.

    
brnfck-qwen3.5-0.8b runs Qwen3.5-0.8B, a newer generation than Qwen2.5. It has the same kind of layers as Qwen3.8-27B, a much larger model: 18 of its 24 layers are Gated DeltaNet, a memory of fixed size, and the other 6 look back at every earlier word piece, one after every three DeltaNet layers. Compare the models explains that kind of layer. Ask it yourself on that page runs the model in floating point with its DeltaNet layers in whole numbers: close to the program, but not what it computes. The program follows its own whole-number recipe exactly.

What to expect

    
    
      
  • Answers can be wrong. These are small models, and running one in brainfuck does not make it smarter.
    • 
      
  • Each question starts fresh. A question is at most 200 characters. The program does not remember earlier ones; the list on the page is only a record of this visit. brnfck-qwen3.5-0.8b cannot yet clear its memory after an answer, so the Chatbot loads it again for each question.
    • 
      
  • The numbers were rounded. Brainfuck has whole numbers only, so each model was converted to whole-number arithmetic. brnfck-smollm2-135m, brnfck-qwen2.5-0.5b and brnfck-qwen2.5-1.5b then pick the same next piece of text as the original model in 94.8%, 96.6% and 97.2% of the tested cases. For brnfck-qwen3.5-0.8b it is 95.1%: its whole-number version, which the program follows exactly, against the original at 225 positions of its answers. That measures agreement with the original, not whether an answer is true.
    • 
      
  • It is heavy. The first question downloads the program, and the tab needs the memory in the table. No phone can run any of the five.
